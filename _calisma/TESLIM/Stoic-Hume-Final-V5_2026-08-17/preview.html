01Final thesis statement

This article does not show that first-order logic cannot represent grounding, modality, or epistemic normativity. It shows that, for the explicitly specified extensional signature L₀ and class of admissible reconstructions 𝒦, the intended explanatory relation between custom-production and the absence of rational warrant is not fixed by the L₀-reduct; representing it requires additional structure whose philosophical interpretation remains independently contestable.

02The formal core

The minimal many-sorted language L₀ has sorts IContB and the predicates KatRep GraspAssentBel CausalCustomJust StoicEp. The enriched language L⁺ adds Fact ⊆ ContcustFact nonjustFactGObtains.

Humean mechanism

M₀a  ∀b∀p ( Causal(b,p) → Custom(b) )
M₀b  ∀b∀p ( Causal(b,p) → Bel(b,p) )

Exclusion readings

T₁  ∀b∀p ( Causal(b,p) → ¬Just(b,p) )
T₂ = H-I  ∀b∀p ( (Custom(b) ∧ Bel(b,p)) → ¬Just(b,p) )
B₀ (bridge)  ∀b∀p ( (Custom(b) ∧ Bel(b,p)) → Causal(b,p) )

Stoic minimal skeleton

(S)  ∀b ( StoicEp(b) → ∃i∃p [ Kat(i) ∧ Rep(i,p) ∧ Grasp(b,i) ∧ Assent(b,p) ] )

Reification axioms (O₁–O₃)

O₁  Obtains(custFact(b)) ↔ Custom(b)
O₂  Obtains(nonjustFact(b,p)) ↔ ¬Just(b,p)
O₃  G(x,y) → Obtains(x) ∧ Obtains(y)
target  G( custFact(b), nonjustFact(b,c) )

03Proposition 1 — strength relation

Prop 1  T₂ ∧ M₀ ⊨ T₁   ✓ holds  ·  T₁ ∧ M₀ ⊭ T₂   ✗ non-entailment (unique countermodel)

Purely comparative and conditional: it establishes neither that Hume endorsed T₂, nor that the causal–custom relation is correctly represented by M₀, nor that T₁ exhausts Humean scepticism.

Bridge collapse (★)

Prop 1 / bridge  T₁ ∧ M₀ ∧ B₀ ⊨ T₂   ✓ 0 falsifiers

If the bridge axiom B₀ is accepted, the two exclusion readings collapse to the same model class (T₁ ≡ T₂). The countermodel to Prop 1 is the single point where the bridge breaks.

Characterization ★  M ⊨ T₁ ∧ M₀ ∧ ¬T₂  ⟺  M ⊨ ∃b∃c [ Custom(b) ∧ Bel(b,c) ∧ Just(b,c) ∧ ¬Causal(b,c) ]

05Proposition 2 — implicit definability failure over 𝒦

Prop 2  ∃ ℳ₁⁺, ℳ₂⁺ ∈ 𝒦 : ℳ₁⁺↾L₀ = ℳ₂⁺↾L₀  ∧  ℳ₁⁺ ⊨ G(custFact(b), nonjustFact(b,c))  ∧  ℳ₂⁺ ⊭ G(custFact(b), nonjustFact(b,c))

Same L₀-reduct, different grounding extension (G₁ = {target}, G₂ = ∅). The pair is in the admissible class 𝒦 (M₀ + sortal + O₁–O₃; deliberately no bridge axiom and no structural principles on G). Three auxiliary results:

  • Definitional irreducibility — no L₀-formula is equivalent to the grounding atom.
  • Stability under arbitrary L₀-theories — the separation survives any L₀-theory (this is the non-triviality of the result, Gate 1.5 T6).
  • Definability qualification — explicit vs. implicit definability, with the Beth (1953) anchor.

Backbone: Lemma (L₀-reduct invariance) — structural induction showing G, Obtains, custFact, nonjustFact are not in L₀, so L₀-truth depends only on the common reduct.

06V5 additions — encoding sensitivity (executed)

The Stoic modal clause is formalized two ways and the underdetermination result is tested under both (encoding_sensitivity_check.py, frozen output encoding_sensitivity_output.txt):

L₀^A (minimal)  Kat(i)  —  modal content M undetermined for 16 / 16 base assignments
L₀^B (decomposition)  Kat(i) ↔ Veridical(i) ∧ SourceMatch(i) ∧ □ₛᵣ꜀ ¬FalseSource(i)  —  M undetermined for 6 / 10 admissible assignments, determined for 4 / 10 (kataleptic case and its direct negation, forced by the decomposition axiom)

Verdict: encoding-sensitive in degree, robust in existence — under both encodings the extensional base alone fails to fix the modal content; where L₀^B determines it, the determination comes from the modal resource written into the theory. The sensitivity is itself a finding (hermeneutic dependence of the formalization), not concealed.
